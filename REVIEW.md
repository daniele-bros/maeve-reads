# Review of Maeve Reads

Two passes over the live game: an early-literacy review, then a kids’-game review. Flows below are the rounds **after** the fixes in this change. Each one was played in Chrome with touch emulation.

## Lens 1 — early literacy

Ranked by impact.

1. **Encoding games hid the goal.** Sound-hop kept the picture invisible until the path was finished, and the word train and letter-build screens never showed a picture at all. A four-year-old was spelling a word she had not been shown. Encoding needs the meaning and the spoken word first (picture + audio), then the letters. Fixed: the picture and the word clip are on screen before she can move a piece.
2. **The present game hid every picture.** She had to open a box to learn what the word meant, so the reveal was the task. A reveal is only a bonus after the match is already possible. Fixed: the three pictures and the printed word are visible, the word is spoken, and confetti is the bonus.
3. **Some mini-game words used letters she has not been taught** (`sun` needs u, `pig` and `dog` need g, a bubble answer was b). Those items asked her to read code from a later chapter. Fixed: those rounds now use only s, a, t, p, i, n, c, k, e, h, r, m, d.
4. **A wrong pop did not show the right balloon.** She could keep tapping with no model. Fixed: the matching balloon is highlighted and the sound plays again; a second miss completes it gently and does not count as mastery.
5. **Letter intros used to put the letter name in the spoken line** (“Say k with me” on a c card). That is fixed already: the card shows the letter being taught, the sentence has no bare letter, and the phoneme clip plays the sound. Speech refuses a one-letter utterance.
6. **Scope is sound.** Chapter 1 is ear work, then s a t p i n, then sat and a small word set. Chapter 2 adds c/k, e, h, r, m, d in pairs, with guided blending, then open blending, then reading, spelling, and short lines that use only that code plus a, the, and is.
7. **Mastery still gates the teaching path** (about 4 of the last 5 first tries). The playful games are free review so she is not locked out of them. Back, Retry, and Next do not write a score.
8. **What is still thin.** Stop sounds are short synthesized puffs, so /k/ and /t/ are clean of a vowel but not as clear as a human voice. Free games mix the whole Chapter 2 set instead of waiting for each new letter. Both are noted, not blockers.

## Lens 2 — kids’ game

Ranked by impact.

1. **No reliable way out.** Robot talk could sit on a hidden choice or a voice that never finished. Fixed earlier and kept: choices show immediately, and the corner bar can always move on.
2. **Build games did not look like a goal.** A path with empty stones and no picture feels like a puzzle with no picture on the box. The picture is now the first thing in the round.
3. **Drag with no tap, and a text Next in the middle of the screen,** made it easy for her to skip by accident and hard to finish a drag. Next now lives in a corner bar. Pieces are large. A wrong drop slides back and Pip pulls a face. It does not cost a point.
4. **Juice is still light** next to Khan Academy Kids or Teach Your Monster to Read: a float, a bounce, confetti, a goofy Pip. Enough to mark success. Not a carnival. That is the right trade for a 4-year-old who is supposed to be reading, not hunting prizes.
5. **She does not have to read the instructions.** Pip speaks once at the start. The corner icons are arrows, a circular arrow, and a house. The header speaker repeats the target sound or word.
6. **A grown-up is still useful for the first Play tap and for switching chapters.** After that she can tap the glowing game. The corner bar is for the adult when a round goes wrong.

## Round flows

What she sees and hears first, what she does, the feedback, and what comes next.

### Chapter 1

**Rhymes.** Pip says two words rhyme, with the pictures. She taps the star. Then she hears “Which one rhymes with cat?” and sees three pictures. She taps or says the match. A miss removes a wrong picture and plays it again. A second miss points to the right one. Next round, or a sticker after the set. Corner Next skips without a score.

**First sounds.** She hears “Sun starts with sss” and the /s/ clip, with a sun picture. Then a sound clip and three pictures. Same miss rules. The sound is the goal, and it plays before she needs to tap.

**Robot talk.** She hears the sounds one by one (short clips, not letter names), then the real word on the model round, which moves on by itself. On her turn the three pictures are already there. She hears the separate sounds once, then taps or says the word. Next is in the corner if the voice stalls.

**Letter sound (s, a, t, p, i, n, and the Chapter 2 letters).** The big letter and the keyword picture are on screen. Pip says “Listen. Like cat.” and the phoneme clip plays. She is not told a letter name. “Say this sound with me” shows that same letter. Then she taps the letter that matches the clip, or the picture that starts like the keyword.

**Blend (sat, then the longer word sets).** The letters are on screen. Guided rounds light the next letter. She taps each sound, then swoops, and hears the normal word (“cat”, not “kay-aat”). Then she picks the picture. Later rounds let her swoop without tapping every letter. The picture is the meaning check after she has looked at the letters.

**Read.** The printed word is on screen. She can tap it to hear the separate sounds. Three pictures are already visible. She taps or says the match.

**Spell.** The picture and the spoken word come first. Empty slots and letter cards are under them. She taps or drags the letters in order. A wrong card wobbles back. Two misses light the letter she needs. No point is lost for a skip.

**Stories.** She sees a, the, and is, and hears those word clips, not letter names. Then a short line such as “a red hat”, spoken as words, with the picture choices already showing.

### Chapter 2 games

**Pop / Bubbles.** She hears the sound first. Three balloons or bubbles with letters are on screen. She pops the match. A miss highlights the right one and plays the sound again. A second miss pops it for her and does not count as mastered.

**Feed the monster.** The picture is in front of the monster and the word is spoken before the cards will move. She drags or aims the printed word into the monster. Wrong card returns. Confetti on a hit.

**Train.** Picture and spoken word first. She drags letters into the cars in order. The right letter snaps and bounces. A wrong drop returns and the letter she needs is highlighted.

**Sound path.** Same: picture and spoken word first, then she drags s, a, t onto the stones in order. Finishing plays the whole word again and celebrates. The picture was never a prize she had to earn before she knew the word.

**Fish.** The picture sits in the net and the word is spoken. Three word-fish sit in a row beside it, so none can hide under the net. She drags the matching word to the picture. The answer is always one of the three.

**Mystery.** Three pictures are face-up. The word is printed and spoken. She drags that word onto the matching picture. Sparkle is the bonus. She never has to guess what is inside a closed box.

**Bubble Pop Reader.** This one is reading, so Pip does not say the word first. One word, then two, then three, show at the top, and the current word is large. Bubbles with pictures drift in the water. She pops them in order. The right pop makes a pop, a sparkle, and says that word. A wrong bubble wobbles back, with no penalty. Bubbles stay in the tank and loop, and every word in the list has a picture on screen. After a few seconds a hint button can say the current word. Twelve words, then a big finish and Play again.

## Session shape

Teaching rounds are about five tries. Drag and match games are about eleven. Pop games are about nine. Bubble Pop Reader is twelve words. Then a sticker, Play again, or stop. Back, Retry, and Next do not change mastery. Retry throws away a stuck sound, a stuck drag, and a hung microphone, then plays the prompt again on the same item. A content check builds 500 sessions of every game before the site deploys.
